# Roadmap

Version 1 ships 35 skills in six blocks, and [README.md](README.md#skills) lists them.

## Not in version 1

Version 2 takes both topics below. Neither is missing by oversight, and the reason differs in
each case.

| Topic | Why it is not here |
|---|---|
| Calls and voice | The rules that govern outbound calling reach into the steps themselves. Each regime would need its own version of the mechanic, and writing that takes a pass over regulatory sources that version 1 does not make. |
| AI inside the program | The topic cuts across every other skill and belongs whole to none of them. A file that names the idea without the operating sequence would not clear the bar every other file here clears. |

## Outside the territory

Nothing below is queued for a later version. The library starts where acquisition ends, so paid
advertising and ad platforms, search, PR, social publishing and brand all sit outside it.

One case touches that edge on purpose. `segmentation` describes exporting a segment to an ad
platform, and it appears there to mark where the library stops rather than as a mechanic you run.

## On numbers

A skill never invents a number, and the library carries no market benchmarks: no figure for the
rate other companies report as normal.

The library leaves them out on purpose. Whoever publishes a rate measures it on their own platform,
audience and definition, and the figure ages with the report that carries it. Each skill says why
for its own control metric: some published figures use a different denominator or window, others
depend so much on category, price and audience that another business's value tells you nothing
about yours. If you bring an outside figure, `metric-definitions` shows you how to check whether it
measures the same thing.

The numbers that do appear come in five kinds, and every skill tells you which kind you are reading:

- **A legal or channel rule.** A reply window, or the statutory deadline for honoring an
  unsubscribe. The skill names what sets the rule, and who the rule does not cover.
- **A quantity from the math.** Set alpha to 0.05 and you have 95% confidence. It follows from the
  definition, so it needs no source.
- **A parameter you compute.** Twice your own median interpurchase interval; the top 15% of your
  base by order value. The skill hands you the recipe, not the answer.
- **A named starting point.** A default you use until you have your own history. The skill says
  where the default applies, and what you replace it with once your own data arrives.
- **A worked example.** Numbers made up to show a calculation. The skill labels them as made up.

The library also leaves out a bare threshold that means "this is normal", with no source, no
math and no recipe behind it. That is a market benchmark with its source removed.
